Slide 05 / 13

NLP Preprocessing Pipeline (5 Steps)

1. Clean

Lowercase & remove symbols.

"apples new iphone 999"
2. Tokenize

Split into words.

['apples', 'iphone']
3. Stopwords

Drop 'the', 'is', 'at'.

['apples', 'iphone']
4. Lemmatize

running -> run.

['apple', 'iphone']
5. Vectorize

Words into numbers.

[0.42, 0.61, 0.28]
💡 Simple Explanation

Computers can't read English. We have to clean the text, chop it into words, remove useless filler words, and turn the words into numbers.

🌍 Real-World Example

Like preparing vegetables to cook. You wash them (clean), chop them (tokenize), throw away the stems (stopwords), and measure them (vectorize) before putting them in the pan.

Slide 06 / 13

The 4 AI Models Tested

NB
Naive Bayes (Simple guesser)
Uses basic probability. Multiplies chances of each word belonging to a category (0.5 ms).
⭐
Linear SVM (Finds boundaries)
Draws a straight dividing line in 10,000-word space. 96.4% accurate and super fast (1.2 ms).
MLP
Neural MLP (Mini brain)
Artificial neural network with 128 neurons. Learns non-linear word patterns (95.1% acc).
BERT
DistilBERT (Supercomputer)
Deep learning transformer (66M weights). Reads sentences bidirectionally to understand context.
💡 Simple Explanation

We tested four different ways for the AI to learn.

🌍 Real-World Example

Imagine four students taking a test: Naive Bayes just guesses based on what it remembers; SVM draws a straight line in the sand to separate answers; MLP uses a complex brain; and BERT reads the entire textbook and remembers every detail.

Dataset & Evaluation

• BBC News Corpus: 2,225 genuine news articles.
• 5 Balanced Categories: Sport, Business, Politics, Tech, Entertainment.
• 80 / 20 Train-Test Split: 80% (1,780) for training, 20% (445) for testing.
• Zero Data Leakage: Test articles kept completely hidden during training.
💡 Simple Explanation

We used a real dataset of 2,225 BBC articles. We taught the AI using 80% of them, and saved 20% to test if it actually learned.

🌍 Real-World Example

Like a teacher giving you practice questions (80%) to study, and then giving you a final exam (20%) with new questions to see if you really understand the topic.

Slide 09 / 13

Error Analysis (Confusion Matrix)

• Best Categories: Sport (99.0% - 101/102 correct) and Entertainment (96.1% - 74/77 correct).
• Hardest Boundary: Politics vs. Business (4 articles confused due to shared words like 'tax', 'budget', 'economy').
• The Solution (Bigrams): We trained the AI on two-word phrases (like 'corporate tax' vs 'tax policy') to tell them apart.
• Total Errors: Only 16 articles out of 445 were misclassified across the entire test set.
💡 Simple Explanation

The AI was great at sports. But it got confused between Politics and Business because they use similar words. We fixed this by teaching it phrases instead of just single words.

🌍 Real-World Example

If an article says "The Chancellor announced the new tax budget." Is it Politics or Business? Even a human gets confused. By looking at the phrase "tax budget" together, the AI understands it better.

Slide 10 / 13

Explainable AI (No More Black Boxes)

✔ Top Keywords Saliency: Shows the exact words that caused the prediction (e.g. 'wembley' = 28%, 'processor' = 35%).
✔ Platt Scaling: Converts raw mathematical margin distances into honest percentage probabilities (e.g. 98.4%).
✔ Low Confidence Warnings: Warns the user when an article touches multiple topics and confidence is below 45%.
💡 Simple Explanation

The AI doesn't just give an answer; it shows exactly which words it used to make the decision. If it's confused, it warns the user.

🌍 Real-World Example

A doctor shouldn't just say, "You are sick." They should say, "You are sick because your temperature is 102." Our AI acts like the good doctor—it shows its evidence.
